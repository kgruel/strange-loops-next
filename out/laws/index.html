What the laws don't promise

Every law is about a given set of records. None says the set is complete, current, trusted or well dated. These are the edges, and the model's own simplifications, from protocol/laws.md as written.

Absence isn't visible.

A reader that hasn't synced a successor sees the record it replaces as current, and can't count what it doesn't know exists. The laws promise convergence, not freshness.

Torn reads.

A reader must not see half a changeset: segments close per changeset with an atomic rename, and readers ignore a partial last line.

Time is the writer's word.

Content order breaks ties by claimed times, so a skewed clock picks a fork's winner. Every head of a fork is still shown.

Trust is step 3.

Nothing here checks signatures or standing; the laws hold for whatever a reader admits.

A checkpoint is a set.

"Since" means segments not yet seen, not an offset, or a late segment is missed.

Simplifications in the model

An id is its 32-byte digest read as a number, most significant byte first, and written as layer 0 writes one (r: and base32). The digests are made up: nothing yet connects these records to layer 0's real ones, so nothing yet proves a fold record holds the times its envelope was checked with.

The payload is value, n and named fields.

Fold records carry their times as text with what each reads as (Stamp, Claim); a record's other envelope fields are as written, not read from an envelope.

Text is a list of lines; splitting a file into lines is outside the model.
