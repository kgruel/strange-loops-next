Laws

67 laws, each a theorem Lean checks on every build, or a rule held by construction, or, where its card says so, partly pinned by vectors instead of proved. They are grouped here by the question they answer, in plain sentences. What proves each one, the vectors that pin it and what its proof rests on are on the evidence page.

A law is one sentence

Name what may vary, and name what doesn't move when it does. Arrival order leaves a count, a sum and a state where they were. Repeats leave a winner or an extremum alone, but they move a count or a sum: those are limits. Holding fewer payloads leaves the heads alone, and an answer that needs a payload can turn unknown or unread. Spelling a time another way leaves a test's answer alone.

Question 2 of 8

Which record is current, and what if two disagree?

7 laws:=Invariance 1<Order 3⇔Exactness 3

  1. The winner is a head, a record nothing about its thing names in prev, and newer than every other head by content order: span, then emitted, then id.

    <Orderlatest-by.orderEvidence

  2. Any two records with different ids order one way: by span, then emitted, then id. Never by how a time is spelled.

    <Ordercontent orderEvidence

  3. A record is refused exactly when it isn't entitled to its prev, and that depends only on the set.

    ⇔Exactnessadmit.setEvidence

  4. Every held record an admitted record's prev names shares a thing with it, and may be replaced by it.

    A prev naming a record the reader doesn't hold is admitted: the reader can't judge it. A writer refuses a prev naming a record it doesn't hold (prev-unknown); a reader that lacks it admits the record.

    ⇔Exactnessadmit.no-crossEvidence

  5. Change the records about other things, once records are settled and admitted, and this thing's state stays.

    Admission and erasure read records about other things, so this starts after them.

    =Invariancething.localEvidence

  6. A slot over heads returns only records that are a head of some thing, that from takes and every test holds for.

    A record about several things can be superseded in one and still head another.

    ⇔Exactnessspec.after-supersessionEvidence

  7. Spans order by start, then end, an open end last: every store orders them the same.

    <Orderoccurred.orderEvidence

Question 4 of 8

What if the same thing is written two ways?

10 laws:=Invariance 7<Order 1⇔Exactness 2

These laws cover the order of, and repeats in, a record's about, prev and erases; the order of an object's or a payload's members; how a time is spelled; the order of a where map's tests; and the order of a batch's drafts. A spelling none of them names is not promised the same.

  1. Change how a time is spelled, the record's or the test's, and the answer stays.

    =Invariancewhere.timeEvidence

  2. Change the order of a where map's tests, and its answer stays.

    =Invariancewhere.setEvidence

  3. An acyclic batch has one meaning: any two assignments of ids to its labels that both fit it agree.

    That the batch resolver returns this meaning, whatever the drafts' order, is checked on every batch vector in every rotation of its drafts, not proved.

    ⇔Exactnessbatch.meaningEvidence

  4. Change the order of about, prev or erases, and repeats in them, and the id stays.

    =Invariancerecord.setsEvidenceTry it

  5. Change the order of an object's members, their names distinct, and its canonical bytes stays.

    =Invariancerecord.member-orderEvidenceWithout its premise

  6. Any two distinct strings order one way, by UTF-16 code units.

    <OrderUTF-16 orderEvidence

  7. Change the order of a written payload's members, and its commitment stays.

    =Invariancewrite.member-orderEvidence

  8. Every point the reader accepts becomes the span the grammar gives it, alone or open-ended, and two such points become one span if the second starts no earlier. No span ends before it starts.

    Which texts are points is the grammar itself, pinned by vectors, not proved.

    ⇔Exactnessoccurred.spanEvidence

  9. Change a qualifier on a date, ?, ~ or %, and the span stays.

    Instants take no qualifier.

    =Invarianceoccurred.qualifierEvidenceTry it

  10. Change trailing zeros in a fraction of a second, and the moment stays.

    =Invarianceoccurred.momentEvidence

Question 6 of 8

What is refused, and how is it said?

6 laws:=Invariance 1⇔Exactness 5

  1. A label given twice, used but not given, or in a cycle is refused; so is a draft write refuses, and a prev not held or about other things.

    ⇔Exactnessbatch.refuseEvidence

  2. Change the order writing finds its refusals in, and the refusal reported stays.

    Proved for writing's refusals; the order across stages (labels, then writing, then prev) is by construction, pinned by vectors.

    =Invariancebatch.refuse-orderEvidence

  3. Malformed text is refused, not guessed at: a section left unclosed, a body line that starts with ::: without being the closing fence, and lines outside any section.

    ⇔Exactnesslens.refuseEvidence

  4. A bad envelope, a salt that isn't 16 bytes, a repeated member name or a number past ±(253 − 1) is refused with a stated reason.

  5. write succeeds exactly when every check passes, and what it returns carries the proof.

    ⇔Exactnesswrite.checkedEvidence

  6. A text the reader doesn't accept is refused with occurred-not-span, never guessed at.

    ⇔Exactnessoccurred.refuseEvidence

What the laws don't promise

The laws about folds, views and specs are each about a given set of records. None says the set is complete, current, trusted or well dated. These are the edges, and the model's own simplifications, from protocol/laws.md as written.

Absence isn't visible.

A reader that hasn't synced a successor sees the record it replaces as current, and can't count what it doesn't know exists. The laws promise convergence, not freshness.

Torn reads.

A reader must not see half a changeset: segments close per changeset with an atomic rename, and readers ignore a partial last line.

Time is the writer's word.

Content order breaks ties by claimed times, so a skewed clock picks a fork's winner. Every head of a fork is still shown.

Trust is step 3.

Nothing here checks signatures or standing; the laws hold for whatever a reader admits.

A checkpoint is a set.

"Since" means segments not yet seen, not an offset, or a late segment is missed.

Simplifications in the model

An id is its 32-byte digest read as a number, most significant byte first, and written as layer 0 writes one (r: and base32). The digests are made up: nothing yet connects these records to layer 0's real ones, so nothing yet proves a fold record holds the times its envelope was checked with.

The payload is value, n and named fields.

Fold records carry their times as text with what each reads as (Stamp, Claim); a record's other envelope fields are as written, not read from an envelope.

Text is a list of lines; splitting a file into lines is outside the model.
