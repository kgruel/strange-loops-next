Laws

67 laws, each a theorem Lean checks on every build, or a rule held by construction. Most say one thing: change this, and that stays. The rest take one of five other shapes. Every sentence here is joined to the theorem that proves it, the vectors that pin it, and the laws its proof rests on.

A law is one sentence

Name what may vary, and name what doesn't move when it does. Arrival order, repeats, which payloads a reader holds, how a time is spelled: the answer stays.

Tap the sentence for the next law.

Change folding in parts or all at once, and the state stays.

incremental = batch

Six shapes

Every law takes one of six shapes, a closed set like the folds. The last shape matters as much as the first: it is where the guarantees stop, said as plainly as where they hold.

Invariance31

Change this, and that stays.

What you may vary without moving the answer. Most laws are this one.

Round trip6

There and back gives what you started with.

Render then parse, parse then print: nothing is lost on the way.

Refinement4

With less in, an answer stays or becomes unknown. It never turns wrong.

What a reader missing payloads is promised, and how it is told.

Order5

Any two compare one way, and exactly one holds.

Strict total orders and complements: no ties, no gaps.

Exactness18

Accepted exactly when it fits; anything else is refused with a reason.

Where the line is, stated as an if and only if, or a refusal that names itself.

Limit3

This one moves, and here is who accounts for it.

What doesn't hold, said as plainly as what does.

What rests on what

A line from one law down to another says a proof of the first uses a theorem of the second, directly or through lemmas no law names. Lean recorded every one of them; lake exe proofs reads them out of the checked proofs.

The stack only rests downward: the build fails if a proof ever leans on a layer above its own.

Point at or tap a law to follow it.

Where the laws stop

Every law is about a given set of records. None says the set is complete, current, trusted or well dated. These are the edges, from protocol/laws.md as written.

Limits

count.repeats

Deliver a record twice and the count moves: it is counted again. So the store must hand each id over once.

sum.repeats

Deliver a record twice and the sum moves: it is added again. So the store must hand each id over once.

heads.why

Max can't follow prev: no step from its state and the next record keeps the current maximum. So folds over heads keep LatestBy's state instead.

What these laws don't promise

Absence isn't visible.

A reader that hasn't synced a successor sees the record it replaces as current, and can't count what it doesn't know exists. The laws promise convergence, not freshness.

Torn reads.

A reader must not see half a changeset: segments close per changeset with an atomic rename, and readers ignore a partial last line.

Time is the writer's word.

Content order breaks ties by claimed times, so a skewed clock picks a fork's winner. Every head of a fork is still shown.

Trust is step 3.

Nothing here checks signatures or standing; the laws hold for whatever a reader admits.

A checkpoint is a set.

"Since" means segments not yet seen, not an offset, or a late segment is missed.

Simplifications in the model

An id is its 32-byte digest read as a number, most significant byte first, and written as layer 0 writes one (r: and base32). The digests are made up: nothing yet connects these records to layer 0's real ones, so nothing yet proves a fold record holds the times its envelope was checked with.

The payload is value, n and named fields.

Fold records carry their times as text with what each reads as (Stamp, Claim); a record's other envelope fields are as written, not read from an envelope.

Text is a list of lines; splitting a file into lines is outside the model.
