What rests on what

A line from one law down to another says a proof of the first uses a theorem of the second, directly or through lemmas no law names. Lean recorded every one of them; lake exe proofs reads them out of the checked proofs.

The stack only rests downward: the build fails if a proof ever leans on a layer above its own. A law's layer is declared, not derived; that check is all the proofs say about it.

A proof by computation (decide) evaluates definitions and uses no theorem, so it draws no line.

Point at or tap a law to follow it, or a line to see the theorems that make it. Enter, or a second tap, opens the law's card.

The map is laid out when the page is built: the same laws and proofs always draw the same map.

From claim to code

A law is a sentence on the laws page, a row in protocol/laws.md, a theorem Lean checks, and vectors every implementation must pass. The build joins all four and fails when one doesn't meet the next.

A sentence says no more than its theorems: where a premise or a proof's reach needs saying, it follows the sentence. Open a theorem to read exactly what Lean checked.

Laws are grouped by the layer they govern, from the ground up.
