Laws

A law is one sentence

Name what may vary, and name what doesn't move when it does. Arrival order, repeats, which payloads a reader holds, how a time is spelled: the answer stays.

It turns on its own while you aren't reading it; Next and Pause are under it.

Six shapes

Every law takes one of six shapes, a closed set like the folds. The last shape matters as much as the first: it is where the guarantees stop, said as plainly as where they hold.

Try to break it

Each card is a vector the model wrote. Press the button to vary what the law says may vary; the answer underneath is the one the model computed for every variant. Nothing here is computed in your browser, and CI regenerates every vector from the model.

The last card is a limit: there, the answer does move, and the law says who must account for it.

Without the premise

Most laws hold only given something: an id names one record, a test answers, a section isn't dropped. Each card asks the model the same question with the premise and without it. Some inputs here are ones the protocol never produces; each card says which.

Coverage

What evidence each law has: theorems Lean checked, vectors laws.md cites, vectors that name the law themselves, whether those two agree, whether the JS writer runs any of them, a demo, and how many laws it rests on and carries.

A filled cell says the evidence exists, not that it is complete: a law cited by one vector is pinned at one point.

From claim to code

A law is a sentence here, a row in protocol/laws.md, a theorem Lean checks, and vectors every implementation must pass. The build joins all four and fails when one doesn't meet the next.

A sentence says no more than its theorems: where a premise or a proof's reach needs saying, it follows the sentence. Open a theorem to read exactly what Lean checked.

Laws are grouped by the layer they govern, from the ground up.

What rests on what

A line from one law down to another says a proof of the first uses a theorem of the second, directly or through lemmas no law names. Lean recorded every one of them; lake exe proofs reads them out of the checked proofs.

The stack only rests downward: the build fails if a proof ever leans on a layer above its own. A law's layer is declared, not derived; that check is all the proofs say about it.

A proof by computation (decide) evaluates definitions and uses no theorem, so it draws no line.

Point at or tap a law to follow it, or a line to see the theorems that make it. Enter, or a second tap, opens the law's card.

The map is laid out when the page is built: the same laws and proofs always draw the same map.

Where the laws stop

Every law is about a given set of records. None says the set is complete, current, trusted or well dated. These are the edges, from protocol/laws.md as written.
