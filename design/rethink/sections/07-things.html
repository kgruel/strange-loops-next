Things and about

Records are the only thing stored. A thing is what a record is about. Identity moves out of the fold and into the record, through about on the envelope. Tested against every case so far, it holds with no new machinery and six refinements.

layer closed Composition starts here: a vertex reading many stores can only merge meaning if it can tell when two records are about the same thing. It closed on three more decisions:

  • Promotion carries its anchor verbatim when the target store lacks it. Across realms a natural key is content, not a reference (svc:auth@corp-prod says something), so it crosses only by bridge. A public store is a standing bridge, which is how url: keys meet across realms (see the web annotation walk in Horizons).
  • Input resolves against the target store's names first, then home. What a miss does follows the store's maturity (see "Referencing a thing mid-flow" below), and it always happens in the target store, so personal petnames don't leak into team anchors.
  • as drops from the envelope. The name in effect at emitted is a fold over dated name records; the automatic first-emit anchor carries the initial self-name.

A chapter has a lineage just as an observer does: a first record, then later records that fold into its current self. The difference is who reads it. The core folds observers, stores and vertices to decide admission, signatures and writer policy. It never needs to know what a chapter is. Primitives have standing: they can sign, hold a grant, admit a record. A thing is acted on and never acts.

Where identity lives

about is the emitter's claim ("this is about r:x1"). A fold key is the vertex's question ("group these by host"). Both stay. about can point at three sorts of target: a record id that anchors a thing (r:…), a natural key (url:…, host:…, svc:…), or a primitive (sl:…, st:…, vx:…).

Case: a chapter, in facets

holds The fold is LatestBy keyed by kind, once a key may name an envelope field. Renumbering, retitling and splitting a chapter all leave r:a1 intact. Diffs inside prose stay out of the model: git and a future blob store compress similar snapshots underneath.

Case: a homelab incident

holds, and simplifies Linking after the fact needs no selection by state, which retracts a worry from the previous turn. A later link is a new record about the target that refers to the evidence. The view selects by what records carry and follows references, like a join. Nothing ever edits about.

Case: the team platform

holds, with one new parameter resolve-by=<identity vertex> maps each about to its class before grouping. It's the third state reference, after admitted-by and weight-by, and the DAG rule covers it. This is the only place identity needs state.

Discipline in an open store

In a semantic store, convention is what keeps a growing store aligned. Admission happens at read time, so discipline can tighten after a store has grown messy: nothing is rewritten, and records that don't meet the new rules become visible rejections. A schema-first store must be disciplined from day one; this one can be disciplined as it matures.

Referencing a thing mid-flow

Referencing something should cost the word you'd say. What happens when that word isn't a known name follows the store's maturity:

loops emit note --about "auth service" "token refresh races on cold start"

open      about="topic:auth-service"                 // no extra record; echoed back for reuse
declared  component-start name="auth service"        // r:x9, then the note about r:x9
strict    refused: no thing called "auth service"; did you mean auth-svc (r:x1)?
  • Keys converge on their own. Two agents who write topic:auth-service meet without coordinating. Anchors never collide, so minting on every miss makes duplicates: three anchors for one service in the team case above.
  • Minting becomes deliberate when a thing must survive a rename or be told apart from a namesake. It comes with a same-as from the key to the new anchor, and every early record joins the anchor's class through resolve-by. No migration.
  • The start record carries the first name, as an anchor carries a self-name; no separate name record.
  • Only about joins. A record that names its topic only in a payload field (topic=auth) is reached by a vertex grouping on that field, or by a later record about the key that refers to it. resolve-by never reads payloads.
Matching: one equality relation is enough

Linked Data's best-documented failure is owl:sameAs: used loosely for "same", "closely related" and "roughly the same", and global and transitive, so a few sloppy links merged unrelated entities across whole datasets. Here same-as is local and disputable, folded per reader. The other guard is a cheap place to say "related", so nobody overloads "same".

SKOS's mapping relations (exactMatch, closeMatch, broadMatch, narrowMatch, relatedMatch) were adopted as an opt-in vocabulary and then dropped. They mix the families in one kind: equivalence, hierarchy and association. And "related" throws away what was observed: the director meant "SSO gateway is the proxy in front of auth-svc", which is a note about both things, not a tag.

RelationshipRecorded asRead by
Same thing, two anchorssame-as; dispute breaks itthe identity vertex, through resolve-by
Two things in one observationa record about both (about is a set), or a payload referenceevery view of either thing; backlinks
A typed relationship, once it mattersa declared kind with named references: kind "fronts" { ref "proxy" "service" }lenses, the LSP
One thing split from anotherthe new anchors' start records refer to the original (split-from, a payload convention)lenses follow it for ancestry

same-as means "always the same thing", so it can't record a split: joining a monolith to the two services it became would pour every later observation of each into the other. prev stays inside one thing's chain. Observations about the monolith stay about the monolith.

None of it enters the core; same-as isn't even a reserved kind. The core holds only resolve-by's contract: connected components over the pairs a vertex accepts.

Where it strains
  • Choosing natural keys is a modeling decision made at collection. A container id dies with the container; the collector has to pick the stable key (svc:jellyfin@nas). A bad choice can only be repaired afterwards with same-as.
  • Duplicate anchors will pile up in the team store, though fewer in open stores, where a miss writes a key instead of minting. That's expected; a hygiene vertex (name similarity, or Wren proposing matches) feeding the stale review keeps them from piling up unmerged. See Discipline above.
  • resolve-by is more than a lookup. The identity vertex folds edges (LatestBy per pair, latest stance wins); turning edges into classes is a connected-components step that lives in resolve-by. That keeps the fold vocabulary closed, but the step has to be specified as carefully as a fold. A class's display id (lean: the earliest anchor in content order) can change when classes merge. Nothing stores it, and lenses show names, so that's acceptable.
  • Recognizing references by prefix can misfire on a string that only looks like an id. Strict stores declare their reference fields; open stores accept the guess.
  • "Everything about auth-svc" across a large class is an index question. It belongs to places (layer 4), not to the semantics.
