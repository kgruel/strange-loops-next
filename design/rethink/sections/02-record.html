Layer 0: the record

A record is an observation, not a statement about the world. Records are immutable, the only write is append, and the record never learns what anything means. A store is a set of records. Order is derived from what records carry, never from where they landed.

One envelope, two variants. Everything done to records (storing, signing, merging, sending, observing) works the same on both. Only the variant tells a conclusion from an observation.

The envelope convention

A field belongs on the envelope only if the core reads it. Everything else is a payload convention. It's a test applied when a field is proposed, not a law.

Time in the id, or a field?

A time-ordered id (UUIDv7, or AT Protocol's TID) carries emit time inside the identifier. A UUIDv7 is out: it's random, and ticks, verbatim promotion, lineage ids and merge dedup all need the id to be a content hash. The real choice was a time prefix plus a content hash against a plain hash with emitted as a field. In both, the hash covers the time, so they hold the same information; the difference is where it shows.

Everything the prefix buys is about where records live (sorting, locality), which the disposable index already provides. Everything the field buys belongs to the model. emitted passes the envelope convention honestly: the core orders by it.

Set, not sequence

Under "set," the store has no order of its own. Order comes from what records carry. Under "sequence," the store carries receipt order as part of its identity. That was Arrival's choice, and it's the root reason a store needed a single authority to hold its head.

Your original conception: the past happened, so occurred/emitted are primary. Receipt order is where things started going off the rails.

"Ordered twice: as claimed, and as witnessed" now sits entirely inside the record. occurred is as claimed. emitted is as witnessed, by the observer rather than the store. Witness time travels with the record, so every store agrees on it.

Why ingestion isn't "unobserved"

Your proposal was that raw ingestion isn't observed, and becomes observed when attention acts on it. The intuition is right: an RSS item arriving and you reading an essay are different. Two problems with the conclusion:

  • It hollows out the observer. Ingestion is most of the volume. If those records have no observer, every fold has to handle that as a special case.
  • It puts observation in the lens. If running a lens makes records observed, reading writes to the record, and the record depends on which lenses happened to run. That breaks "meaning binds at read."

The resolution: every record is observed, but observers see different things. A collector sees a row ("Safari's database says you visited u at t"). That's hearsay, and the record claims no more than the collector saw. The collector is still an observer, just a dumb one.

Acting on a digest item (reading, marking, dismissing) is your observation. Running the salience lens is mechanism.

Occurred: one field that can be a span

A coarse date is already a span: 2009 means somewhere in [2009-01-01, 2010-01-01). If every value normalizes to [start, end), one field covers instants, coarse dates and spans. Ordering is by start, then end, then emitted, then id.

2026-10-17T09:14:03Zinstant (a degenerate span)
2009coarse: sometime in 2009
2026-10-17T09:14/PT4Ma four-minute visit
2009-07/2011-03a migration that ran about two years
2009~ · 2009? · 2009/..approximate · uncertain · open-ended (EDTF Level 1)

Still to muse on

  • Duration vs imprecision. "Visited for 4 minutes" lasted the whole span. "Decided in 2009" happened at some point inside it. Both order the same, but folds may care (summing attention time vs placing a decision on a timeline). Lean: the core stays out of it and the kind decides at read. This is the most likely place a second field becomes necessary.
  • Claim at write vs derive at read. Lean: occurred on the envelope is the observer's own claim. A declaration may derive or reinterpret time at read, as a lens, without overwriting the claim. For dumb collectors either works.
  • Revised claims. "Actually it was 2011" is a new record about a record. A fold applies it at read.
  • Intervals (overlap, containment) are out of scope for layer 0.
EDTF, ISO 8601-2, RFC 3339

EDTF (Extended Date/Time Format) came out of Library of Congress archival work and was folded into ISO 8601-2:2019. It isn't an RFC. Level 0 is ISO 8601 basics. Level 1 adds approximate, uncertain, unspecified digits (200X), seasons and open interval ends. Level 2 is more elaborate. loc.gov/standards/datetime

RFC 3339 profiles ISO 8601 for internet timestamps. rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3339

The about field and thing identity

about is promoted from a payload ref to an envelope field because composition depends on it: salience groups by thing across three stores.

The hard part is thing identity across stores. The same essay shows up as a feed GUID in RSS, a URL with tracking parameters in Safari, and a post id in likes. The answer is both: a typed natural key normalized per prefix where the world supplies one, and same-as records folded at read where it takes judgment. Worked through in Things.
