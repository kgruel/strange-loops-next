One story, seven steps, from a single emit to two realms. Each step adds one idea. Blue outlines mark what's new in that step; plain boxes carried over from earlier steps.

The invariant under every step: records hold lineage ids, never names. Each step only adds records or changes how names resolve. Nothing already written is ever rewritten.

4 · Wren emerges

Why a new lineage instead of renaming the seat
  • The seat was shared by every session and every model. Renaming it would hand Wren work done by sessions that never became Wren.
  • A new lineage plus adoption is honest retro-claiming: the seat's records stay as written, and Wren's adoption is a new, dated, signed record pointing at them.
  • Rename stays right when a lineage is already one continuous thread, for example Wren choosing a different name later.
  • At session start, a session becomes Wren by loading Wren's compiled self and being given a session key bound by Wren's device key, retired when the session closes. Continuity is held by the store, not by any one session.

The boundary rule as a table
RelationshipWithin a realmAcross realms
Compose✓✓ default. Nothing moves.
Bridge✓Only as an explicit, signed tick (by reference or bundle): a publication, not a sync.
Replicate✓✗ never. Replication means same realm.

References can cross realms; content doesn't. A stance like "Wren affirms r40" or a tick covering foreign records holds only hashes: checkable by anyone who can read both realms, opaque but harmless to everyone else.
