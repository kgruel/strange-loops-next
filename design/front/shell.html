Records in. Folds and ticks out. Lenses back to text.

Strange Loops keeps what people said as signed records that never change. Everything else is worked out from them: who is current, what was decided, the page you read. Any of it can be thrown away and worked out again.

A record is one sentence, signed.

Every record says who saw something, when, about which thing, and what they saw. Point at any part of the sentence to find it in the record.

Its id is what it says.

A record's id is a hash of the record written one canonical way. Here is record M from the walk below. Make each change and watch which ones the id notices.

Each change is applied on top of the last. Reset to start over.

A toy: real records also carry a salt, a signature and more envelope fields, and are hashed with the protocol's own rules. The behaviour is the same.

A law is a promise in one sentence.

There are 124 of them. Each says what you may change without moving an answer, or exactly where a promise stops. Lean has checked a proof of all but three, and those three hold by how the code is built.

Below, you can rewrite five of the rules they rest on and watch laws break, take a premise away, or read all 124 by the question they answer.
